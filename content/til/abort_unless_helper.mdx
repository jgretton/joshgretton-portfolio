`abort_unless()` throws an HTTP exception when the expression you give it evaluates to `false`. It saves wrapping `abort()` in an `if`, so the check reads as a single guard clause at the top of a controller method.

I used it in [VolleyJunior](/volleyjunior), where route model binding could hand back a player instead of a guardian.

```php title="PHP"
abort_unless($guardian->role === Role::GUARDIAN, 404);
```
